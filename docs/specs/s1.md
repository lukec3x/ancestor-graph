## Goal Capsule

A person can register people, link one parent and one spouse, and see everyone stored on the tree, with the child one generation below the parent. Stop when that holds after every successful save. A parent cycle, a child of two parents, and spouses on different generations are stored and shown. Only their generation ranks stay with S4.

## Product Contract

### Summary

The page shows the whole stored graph. From the panel, a person registers people, links a parent, and links a spouse. A person on the tree shows their name only. A child of one parent, when that link does not close a parent cycle, sits one generation below that parent. A spouse link does not move either person to another generation. When linking spouses, the person can set a start date and an end date, and either may be empty. A date that is present shows on the spouse link. The union is not its own node. After each successful save, the tree is the graph just read.

### Problem Frame

The graph, the writes, and the rank are already decided. There is no screen and no API yet, so a person cannot register anyone or see a parent and a spouse. This slice is the first time that outcome is true.

### Requirements

R1. A person can register a person. The registered person has a name, a sex, a birth date, a death date, and notes. The sex is `female`, `male`, or `unknown`. The sex control starts at `unknown`, and the person can change it before registering. The name, the notes, and either date may be empty. A date that is present is a calendar date.

R2. A person can link two different, already registered people as parent and child, without a union. The panel asks for the parent and the child as two labeled choices. The parent link is visible between them and shows no union date. On the tree, the child sits one generation below that parent when the child has no other parent and the link does not close a parent cycle. A second parent and a parent cycle keep the generations R10 leaves for S4.

R3. A person can link two different, already registered people as spouses. The link creates their union. The spouse link is visible between them and does not move either person to another generation. A present start date shows on that link as `start YYYY-MM-DD`. A present end date shows as `end YYYY-MM-DD`. When both are present, the start comes first. An empty union date is omitted. The union is not its own node. This slice is satisfied when those two people already share a generation.

R4. When the page opens, and after each successful registration, parent link, or spouse link, the tree shows the graph just read. That graph is every stored person, union, and link, including people the last write did not touch.

R5. The graph the page reads carries neither a generation rank nor coordinates. The page computes the generation for display.

R6. When nothing is stored, the tree shows no people, no unions, and no links.

R7. The panel chooses the people for a parent link or a spouse link from people already registered. For a spouse link, the order of the two people does not matter. A click on a person on the tree does not open that person's fields in this slice.

R8. When linking spouses, the panel shows a start date and an end date. Either date may be empty. A date that is present is a calendar date. Those dates are stored on the union the link creates.

R9. A person on the tree shows their name and nothing else. An empty name is still a visible person, with no substitute name. The registration panel shows name, sex, birth date, death date, and notes. Those fields are not drawn on the person.

R10. A second parent, a parent cycle, and a spouse link between two people who do not share a generation can be stored. Those people appear on the tree. The page still gives every stored person a generation so the tree can draw them. This slice does not accept those generations and does not refuse those writes.

R11. After a successful registration, parent link, or spouse link, the panel returns to empty. Name, notes, and dates are empty, sex is `unknown` again, and the link choices are cleared. The tree keeps the graph just read.

### Acceptance Examples

1. The store is empty. The tree shows no one.
2. Register Ada with sex `female` and a birth date, and Ben with sex `unknown` and an empty name. Both appear on the same generation. Ada's node shows her name and not her birth date. Ben's node shows no name and is still a person on the tree.
3. Link Ada as Ben's parent. Ben appears one generation below Ada.
4. Register Cy with sex `male` and no parent. Link Ada and Cy as spouses, with empty union dates. Ada and Cy stay on the same generation. The spouse link is visible and shows no dates. The graph read includes their union. Ben stays one generation below Ada.
5. Register Dana. Ada, Ben, Cy, and Dana are all on the tree.
6. Register Eve and Finn, both with no parent. Link them as spouses with a start date and an empty end. They stay on the same generation. The spouse link shows `start` and that date as `YYYY-MM-DD`. It does not show an end. The graph read includes that start date and an empty end.

### Scope Boundaries

- Replacing a person's fields, opening a person to edit, and showing a refused write are S2.
- Attributing a child to a union, and seeing that attribution, is S3. The parent link in this slice does not carry a union.
- The rank for a parent cycle, for a child of two parents, and for spouses whose generations differ is S4. Those writes are stored in this slice and the people are on the tree. This slice does not accept their generations.
- This slice does not delete a person, remove a link, create a union on its own, or update a union after the spouse link.
- This slice does not add accounts, a second tree, GEDCOM, citations, or a second visualization.
- Year-only and approximate dates are not part of registration.
- A repeated link, a parent cycle, and a death date earlier than the birth date remain stored. This slice does not refuse them.
- Refusing a link from a person to themselves is S2.

### Key Decisions

- AD-01. One database is the whole graph. The graph read includes every stored person, union, and link. The tree draws every person and every link. A union is not its own node. No read or write carries a tree id or an account id.
- AD-02. The browser owns generation rank. The graph document has no rank and no coordinates. In this slice a person with no parent shares the top generation, a child of one parent sits one generation below that parent, and a spouse link does not change generation. A parent cycle, a child of two parents, and spouses on different generations are stored and shown. Only their generation ranks stay with S4.
- AD-03. Writes are resources, and a spouse link creates the union. Registration, the parent link, the spouse link, and the graph read follow that contract. The parent link in this slice omits `union_id`. A spouse link is one unordered pair and creates the union.
- AD-06. The canvas draws the last graph it was given. After each successful save the panel reads the graph again and replaces the document the canvas draws. The write result itself is not the graph.
